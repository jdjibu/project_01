# Phase 2: Business and industry analysis

Makita Corporation (6586 JP). IFRS, JPY, fiscal year (FY) ends 31 March; FY2026 = year to 31 Mar 2026.
Company figures are from `data/clean` (Makita earnings releases and audited statements); external facts carry
their URL. All sources retrieved 29 Oct 2026.

## 1. Business model in one paragraph
Makita designs, makes and sells power tools and outdoor power equipment (OPE) for professional users, mostly in
building, construction and maintenance, and increasingly in landscaping, agriculture and forestry. It runs a
battery platform model: a user who owns Makita 18V LXT or 40Vmax XGT batteries can run hundreds of tools on them,
so each battery sold raises the switching cost of the next tool purchase. Makita sells through dealers and
distributors in about 40 countries, backed by its own sales and service subsidiaries. It makes 92.5% of units
outside Japan (FY2026). The reportable segments are geographic because the Board treats the group as a single business.
<br>*Sources: segment note, `202603cfs.pdf` p.18; production ratio, FY3/2026 release p.16.*

## 3. Key drivers
| Driver | Why it matters | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Construction and housing activity (Europe, Japan) | Professional tool demand follows construction volumes | Company commentary FY2026: slow housing and high rates held back demand; non-residential and infrastructure solid |
| Cordless conversion | Battery tools replace corded, pneumatic and engine products | Cordless share of Makita OPE "has now exceeded 90%" (Makita Report 2026, director dialogue) |
| 40Vmax mix | Higher-power, higher-price platform; Makita targets a larger 40Vmax share of cordless sales | 261 40Vmax models in Japan at Apr 2026 vs 233 at Apr 2025 (Makita Report 2026 p.20) |
| FX | 83% of revenue is outside Japan; 1Q FY2027 revenue +10.7% in JPY but flat in local currency | 1Q FY3/2027 release p.3 |
| Channel inventory | Dealer and own-inventory cycles drove the FY2022-24 boom and bust | Inventory peaked at JPY 475bn (FY2022); OP margin fell to 3.7% (FY2023) |
| Tariffs and sourcing | US tariffs on China-made goods; Makita still makes 51.5% of units in China | Makita Report 2026 p.23; 1Q FY2027 release cites a refund of U.S. tariffs |

| **Makita** | **JPY 777.6bn = about US$5.2bn (FY3/2026)** | At FY average 150.67 JPY/USD | FY3/2026 release |

**Assessment.** Makita is the clear number one in Japan and among the leaders in Europe. In North America it is a
sub-scale player behind TTI and Stanley Black & Decker (DeWalt), and its own segment margins show this. TTI is growing
faster (Milwaukee +7.9% in 2025 vs Makita +3.2% in JPY, about flat in local currency), so Makita is likely losing
share in the professional cordless segment globally. Its relative strengths are the breadth of one battery platform
across tools and OPE, a dense service network, and a very strong balance sheet. Its relative weaknesses are a slower
U.S. go-to-market and a later move into 40V high-power tools than TTI's MX Fuel and DeWalt's FlexVolt.

## 5. Industry
**Market size.** Estimates differ widely by scope, so I quote a range rather than one number:

| Estimate | Scope | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US$30-40bn power tools; US$40-50bn OPE | Makita's own estimate | Makita Report 2026 p.22 |
| US$29.7bn (2025) | Power tools | [Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/power-tools-market) |
| US$35.3bn (2025) | Power tools | [Persistence Market Research](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/power-tools-market.asp) |
| US$46.8bn (2025) | Power tools | [Arizton](https://www.arizton.com/market-reports/power-tools-market) |
| About 62% cordless share (2025) | Power tools | [Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/power-tools-market) |

Makita's share of the power-tool market is roughly 13-17% on its own definition (US$5.2bn sales, which also include
OPE and parts, against US$30-40bn). This is an indicative figure only.

**Growth.** Mature, broadly GDP-plus. The listed leaders reported low-to-mid single-digit local-currency growth in 2025
(TTI +4.1% total, Hilti +1.9%, Makita about flat).

**Structure.** A concentrated oligopoly at the professional end (TTI, Stanley Black & Decker, Bosch, Makita, Hilti),
with a long tail of Chinese OEMs (Chervon, Positec) in DIY and private label. The OPE market is still dominated by
engine makers (Husqvarna, Stihl, Toro, Yamabiko), which is Makita's main growth opening.

**Regulation (tailwinds for cordless).**
- California: CARB approved zero-emission standards for engines in most new small off-road equipment (leaf blowers,
  mowers, chainsaws) from model year 2024; generators and large pressure washers follow from model year 2028
  ([CARB, Dec 2021](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/node/16486)).
- EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542: labelling, collection and battery-passport duties that raise compliance costs
  for smaller makers ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)).
- U.S. tariffs: the Supreme Court ruled on 20 Feb 2026 that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs; the Court of International
  Trade ordered refunds to importers of record on 4 Mar 2026
  ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-and-the-supreme-court-ruling)).
  This explains the "refund of U.S. tariffs" in Makita's 1Q FY2027 cost ratio. It is non-recurring, and other tariff
  authorities (for example Section 301 and 232) are unaffected.

**Macro sensitivity.**

| Factor | Exposure | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| JPY | 83% of revenue overseas; 92.5% of units made overseas (China 51.5%, Romania 18.6%, Thailand 8.8%) | A weaker yen raises JPY revenue and costs. Net effect is positive because sales are mainly EUR/USD while costs are mainly CNY/RON/THB. Quantified in Phase 6 |
| Interest rates | Housing and construction demand in Europe and Japan | Higher rates mean lower volumes, as seen in FY2024-26 |
| Commodities | Lithium-ion cells, copper, steel, resin | Gross margin pressure with a lag |
| Tariffs and geopolitics | China production share is still 51.5% | Diversification to Romania, Thailand and Brazil is under way |

## 6. Porter's Five Forces (concise)
| Force | Rating | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | High | Five global leaders compete on platform breadth, power and price; TTI is outgrowing the field |
| Threat of new entrants | Low-moderate | Battery platforms, dealer access and service networks are expensive to build; Chinese OEMs enter at the DIY end |
| Buyer power | Moderate | Large dealers and home centres have leverage. Professional end users are locked into battery platforms |
| Supplier power | Moderate | Lithium-ion cells come from a few Asian suppliers; Makita designs its own packs and motors |
| Substitutes | Low, and falling | Corded and engine tools are the substitutes and are losing share to cordless |

**Moat verdict: narrow.** The battery-platform lock-in and the service network are real and show up in a rising
aftermarket share. But Makita does not have pricing power over TTI. Its operating margin (13.5% in FY2026, 3.7% in
FY2023) is below its own 15% target and has proved cyclical.
